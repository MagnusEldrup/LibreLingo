# Lesson 20: Food Detail: Qado and Qadada

Module: Family Dinner
Suggested skills: `qado_qadada`, `meal_words_detail`
Source material: `Chatbot_1.pdf`, pages 15-16

Focus
Introduce the useful contrast between `qado` as lunch/a meal and `qadada` as the lunch/the meal.

Why this lesson exists
This is a small, concrete definiteness lesson anchored in food. It should improve dinner-table comprehension without becoming a full article/case lecture.

Prerequisites
- Food Practice: Eating and Drinking
- Dinner Vocabulary
- Common phrases

Core vocabulary
- `qado` - lunch / midday meal
- `qadada` - the lunch / the midday meal
- `diyaar` - ready
- `cunayaa` - eat / am eating
- `miiska` - the table
- `keen` - bring
- `rabaa` - want
- `bariis` - rice
- `hilib` - meat

Phrases
- `Waxaan cunayaa qado.` - I am eating lunch.
- `Qadada waa diyaar.` - The lunch is ready.
- `Qadada miiska keen.` - Bring the lunch to the table.
- `Waxaan rabaa qado.` - I want lunch.
- `Qadada waxaan rabaa bariis.` - For lunch, I want rice.
- `Bariis iyo hilib baan rabaa.` - I want rice and meat.

Contrast
- `qado` - lunch / a lunch
- `qadada` - the lunch

Mini-dialogue
- A: `Qadada waa diyaar.` - The lunch is ready.
- B: `Mahadsanid. Waxaan rabaa bariis.` - Thank you. I want rice.
- A: `Bariis iyo hilib miiska keen.` - Bring rice and meat to the table.
- B: `Waa hagaag.` - OK.

Notes for YAML
- Place in or near the `Family Dinner` module.
- Keep this concrete; do not turn it into a broad definiteness lesson yet.
- Reuse dinner vocabulary so the only new conceptual load is `qado` vs `qadada`.
- Include chips prompts for word order and options prompts for recognizing the contrast.
- Teach `qado` first in eating sentences, then `qadada` in "is ready" and "bring it" sentences.
- Avoid adding other article examples in the first version; the win is one reliable contrast.
