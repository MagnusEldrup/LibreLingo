# Lesson 14: Grammar: Present Shapes in Common Verbs

Module: Daily Basics
Suggested skills: `long_vowels_common_verbs`, `present_form_spelling`
Source material: `Chatbot_1.pdf`, pages 8-10; `Chatbot_2.pdf`, pages 4-8

Focus
Make common present-tense shapes feel familiar, especially the difference between a simple/habitual form and a progressive form.

Why this lesson exists
The first transcript shows spelling and vowel-length trouble (`joogaa`, `cunayaa`). The second transcript adds a deeper issue: learners need to know that `cuna` and `cunayaa` are not the same shape. This lesson should teach recognition first and production second.

Prerequisites
- Present tense markers
- `waan` and `waxaan`
- Food and daily-life words

Core vocabulary
- `joogaa` - am staying / located
- `cuna` - eats / eat, general present
- `cunayaa` - is eating / am eating now
- `cabbo` - drink, after `inaan`
- `akhriyaa` - reads / read, general present
- `akhriyayaa` - is reading / am reading now
- `ordaa` - runs / run, general present
- `ordayaa` - is running / am running now
- `jecelahay` - like
- `rabaa` - want

Phrases
- `Waxaan joogaa Copenhagen.` - I am in Copenhagen.
- `Waan cuna.` - I eat.
- `Waan cunayaa.` - I am eating now.
- `Waxaan cunayaa moos.` - I am eating a banana.
- `Waan akhriyaa.` - I read.
- `Waan akhriyayaa.` - I am reading now.
- `Waxaan akhriyayaa buug.` - I am reading a book.
- `Waan ordayaa.` - I am running.
- `Waxaan rabaa shaah.` - I want tea.

Contrast
- `Waan cuna.` - I eat / I usually eat.
- `Waan cunayaa.` - I am eating now.
- `Waan cunay.` - I ate.
- `Waxaan joogaa Denmark.` - I am in Denmark.
- Avoid short forms like `Waxaan jooga Denmark` in beginner practice.

Micro-drills
- `joogaa` - say the long `aa`
- `cunayaa` - present eating
- `akhriyayaa` - present reading
- `ordayaa` - present running

Notes for YAML
- This can be a normal lesson or a grammar checkpoint.
- Use typed short-input prompts to reinforce spelling, with typo tolerance for doubled vowels.
- Do not over-explain phonology. Keep it practical: the long vowel changes meaning and tense.
- Tie the lesson to the app's existing relaxed grading around doubled letters.
- Do not drill full conjugation tables here. Use first person only, plus one recognition item with `wuu` or `way`.
- Challenge flow: recognize contrast -> choose translation -> produce one short sentence.
