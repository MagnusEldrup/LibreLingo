# Lesson 18: Questions: Answer in Full Sentences

Module: Daily Basics
Suggested skills: `questions_full_sentence_answers`, `spoken_response_practice`
Source material: `Chatbot_1.pdf`, pages 5 and 12

Focus
Build confidence answering common questions with complete Somali sentences.

Why this lesson exists
The transcript moved quickly from isolated translation into being able to answer normal questions. This lesson should be a compact response drill that reuses earlier material without adding grammar.

Prerequisites
- Introductions
- Food production
- Past vs present
- `Waxaan rabaa inaan...`

Core vocabulary
- `magacaa` - what is your name?
- `xaggee` - where?
- `joogtaa` - are located / staying
- `maxaad` - what do you?
- `jeceshahay` - like
- `rabtaa` - want
- `tagtay` - went
- `shalay` - yesterday

Questions and model answers
- `Magacaa?` - What is your name?
- `Magacaygu waa Peter.` - My name is Peter.
- `Xaggee joogtaa?` - Where are you?
- `Waxaan joogaa Copenhagen.` - I am in Copenhagen.
- `Maxaad jeceshahay?` - What do you like?
- `Waan jeclahay bun.` - I like coffee.
- `Maxaad rabtaa?` - What do you want?
- `Waxaan rabaa shaah.` - I want tea.
- `Xaggee ayaad tagtay shalay?` - Where did you go yesterday?
- `Shalay waxaan tagay suuqa.` - Yesterday I went to the market.

Mini-dialogue
- A: `Magacaa?`
- B: `Magacaygu waa Peter.`
- A: `Xaggee joogtaa?`
- B: `Waxaan joogaa Copenhagen.`
- A: `Maxaad jeceshahay?`
- B: `Waan jeclahay bun.`

Notes for YAML
- This should be a conversation skill, not self-marked speaking.
- Each turn can show the Somali question and ask for the Somali reply from an English cue.
- Keep expected replies short and full-sentence.
- Reuse this as a checkpoint before freer writing or speaking drills.
- Start with personal answers, then one preference, then one past answer.
- Do not require the learner to invent answers in fixed-answer practice. Give an English cue like "Say: I want tea."
