# Lesson 17: Past vs Present: Eat and Go

Module: Communication and Journey
Suggested skills: `past_vs_present_eat_go`, `simple_time_contrast`
Source material: `Chatbot_1.pdf`, pages 8-11 and 13; `Chatbot_2.pdf`, pages 4-8

Focus
Help learners separate present forms from past forms in common daily sentences.

Why this lesson exists
Both transcripts show the learner mixing present, progressive, and past. This lesson should be deliberately narrow: only time cues plus `cun-` and `tag-`.

Prerequisites
- Present shapes in common verbs
- Time and days
- Past tense markers, or this can serve as the first practical bridge into that table

Core vocabulary
- `maanta` - today
- `imminka` - now
- `shalay` - yesterday
- `had iyo jeer` - always
- `ma` - not, in a negative present sentence
- `laakiin` - but
- `markaa` - then / at that time
- `cuna` - eat, general present
- `cunayaa` - eat / am eating
- `cunay` - ate
- `tagaa` - go, general present
- `tagayaa` - go / am going
- `tagay` - went
- `suuqa` - to the market / at the market
- `qado` - lunch
- `bariis` - rice

Phrases
- `Had iyo jeer waan cuna.` - I always eat.
- `Imminka waan cunayaa.` - I am eating now.
- `Shalay waan cunay.` - Yesterday I ate.
- `Waxaan cunayaa bariis.` - I am eating rice.
- `Shalay waxaan cunay bariis.` - Yesterday I ate rice.
- `Waan tagaa.` - I go.
- `Waan tagayaa.` - I am going now.
- `Shalay waxaan tagay suuqa.` - Yesterday I went to the market.
- `Shalay suuqa ayaan tagay.` - Yesterday I went to the market.
- `Imminka ma cunayo, laakiin markaa waan cunay.` - I am not eating now, but then I ate.

Key contrast
- `cuna` = eats / usually eats
- `cunayaa` = is eating now
- `cunay` = ate
- `tagaa` = goes / usually goes
- `tagayaa` = is going now
- `tagay` = went

Practice prompts
- I eat. -> `Waan cunayaa.`
- I ate. -> `Waan cunay.`
- I eat rice. -> `Waxaan cunayaa bariis.`
- Yesterday I ate rice. -> `Shalay waxaan cunay bariis.`
- I went to the market yesterday. -> `Shalay waxaan tagay suuqa.`

Notes for YAML
- Place after `Past Tense Markers`, or use it as a bridge into that lesson.
- Use time words as strong cues in prompts.
- Accept both `Shalay waxaan tagay suuqa` and `Shalay suuqa ayaan tagay` if answer handling allows.
- Keep the set small; the point is tense contrast, not broad past-tense coverage.
- Introduce the negative line only as a stretch/review item. Do not make `ma cunayo` central unless a separate negation lesson exists.
- Avoid library/reading vocabulary here unless needed for review; it distracts from the tense contrast.
